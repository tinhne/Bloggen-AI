## Ninh Bình: A Small Province with Outsized Significance

**Despite being one of Vietnam's smallest provinces, Ninh Bình boasts a rich history and unique distinctions that resonate both nationally and globally.**

**Historical Context and Administrative Evolution**

Ninh Bình's origins trace back to a coastal marshland. In the 10th century, it rose to prominence as Hoa Lư, the capital of Đại Cồ Việt, Vietnam's first centralized feudal state. The area was known as Phủ Trường Yên in the 13th century and Thanh Hoa ngoại trấn in the 18th century during the Later Lê dynasty. The province of Ninh Bình officially appeared on the map in 1831, during the reign of Emperor Minh Mạng.

Following the August Revolution, Ninh Bình was established as a province in 1945. However, in 1976, it merged with Hà Nam (including Nam Định and Hà Nam) to form Hà Nam Ninh province. In December 1991, the National Assembly decided to separate Ninh Bình from Hà Nam Ninh, and on April 1, 1992, Ninh Bình was officially re-established as a distinct administrative entity.

Currently, Ninh Bình covers an area of 1,387.5 square kilometers with a population of approximately one million people. It is divided into seven administrative units: Hoa Lư City, Tam Điệp City, and the districts of Nho Quan, Gia Viễn, Yên Mô, Yên Khánh, and Kim Sơn.

**Hoa Lư: The Ancient Imperial Capital**

In 968, Đinh Bộ Lĩnh, after quelling the "warlords' era" and unifying the country, ascended to the throne, establishing the Đinh dynasty. He named the country Đại Cồ Việt and chose his homeland, Hoa Lư, as the capital.

Hoa Lư served as the capital for 42 years, witnessing the rise and fall of the Đinh, Early Lê, and Lý dynasties. In 1010, King Lý Thái Tổ moved the capital from Hoa Lư to Thăng Long (present-day Hanoi), marking the end of Hoa Lư's reign as the nation's center.

Despite no longer being the capital, subsequent dynasties (Lý, Trần, Lê, Nguyễn) continued to maintain and expand upon Hoa Lư, constructing temples, tombs, shrines, and pagodas. Today, the Cố đô Hoa Lư (Ancient Capital of Hoa Lư) is a Special National Monument and a core component of the Tràng An World Heritage Site. Visitors can explore historical relics such as the temples and tombs of Kings Đinh Tiên Hoàng and Lê Đại Hành, the Nhất Trụ Pagoda, the Yên Thành communal house, and the Am Tiên Cave.

**Tràng An: A Dual-Designation World Heritage Site**

On June 25, 2014, UNESCO recognized the Tràng An Landscape Complex as a World Cultural and Natural Heritage Site in Doha, Qatar. This prestigious designation made Tràng An the first mixed heritage site in Vietnam and Southeast Asia, acknowledging its outstanding universal value in terms of culture, aesthetic beauty, and geological significance.

The Tràng An World Heritage Site spans 6,226 hectares, with a buffer zone of 6,026 hectares. The area encompasses limestone mountains, wetlands, rice paddies, and residential areas, located entirely within Hoa Lư City.

Recently, an international conference organized by the Ninh Bình Department of Tourism assessed the total economic value (TEV) of the Tràng An World Heritage Site at $213 billion USD, placing it among the world's most valuable heritage sites, alongside iconic landmarks such as the Eiffel Tower ($545 billion USD) and the Colosseum ($114 billion USD).

**Cúc Phương National Park: A Pioneer in Conservation**

Located across the provinces of Ninh Bình, Hòa Bình, and Thanh Hóa, Cúc Phương National Park holds the distinction of being Vietnam's first national park, established in 1960. The park's main entrance and a significant portion of its 22,200-hectare area are situated in Ninh Bình.

Cúc Phương is renowned for its rich biodiversity, representing a typical tropical rainforest ecosystem. It has become a prominent tourist destination in Ninh Bình, attracting visitors with its natural beauty and conservation efforts. From 2019 to 2024, the park has been consistently recognized by the World Travel Awards as Asia's Leading National Park.

**A Short but Significant Coastline**

Ninh Bình is strategically located in the Red River Delta, serving as the southern gateway to northern Vietnam. The province shares borders with Hà Nam to the north, Nam Định to the east and northeast, Hòa Bình to the northwest, Thanh Hóa to the south, and the East Sea.

With a coastline of just 18 kilometers, Ninh Bình has the shortest coastline among Vietnam's provinces. The Kim Sơn district is the only part of the province that borders the sea, sharing boundaries with Nghĩa Hưng district (Nam Định) to the north and Nga Sơn district (Thanh Hóa) to the south. Notably, the Kim Sơn coastline is expanding due to land accretion, extending seaward by hundreds of meters each year.

Ninh Bình is included in the Northern Coastal region development plan by the Vietnamese government. It is also designated as one of eight provinces slated to become centrally-governed cities.

The Tràng An World Heritage Site has elevated Ninh Bình's global profile, earning it recognition as a "heritage city" (Hoa Lư), a UNESCO-designated city, and a prominent tourist destination alongside other Vietnamese gems such as Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, Hue, Hoi An, and Ha Long.

Since its re-establishment in 1992, Ninh Bình has experienced rapid economic growth and boasts the highest budget revenue among the three provinces of Hà Nam, Nam Định, and Ninh Bình. In 2024, Ninh Bình's budget revenue reached 21 trillion VND, surpassing Hà Nam's 17 trillion VND and Nam Định's 13 trillion VND, solidifying its position as an economically vibrant province with a rich historical and natural legacy.
